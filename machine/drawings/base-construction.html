Base Construction

Two grids that locate and constrain all five legs, three of which the box lands on. It is built BEFORE the box, and the legs before it — the box cannot be lowered onto tenons that are not located yet. All sizes in mm, all generated from the dimension registry.

One sheet of 18 is already cut into 20 strips of 90, which is exactly the five legs. That sheet is spent and the sheet count below is for everything else. The offcut past the leg lengths is the test material and the slot spacers — a spacer cut from the stock that will fill the slot is a slot that fits by construction.

Every 36 member is two laminations of 18, listed here as the 18 pieces that actually get cut. The 36 is not a choice — it is what fills a leg's slot, which is the middle two laminations of a four-strip leg.

🔴 The two central laminations come off ONE 5 × 5, and that is what cutting them 6 shorter than the back wall buys. A 5 × 5 trims to 1520.8, and with the 1.6 kerf between the two pieces each may be at most 759.6 tall. At the back wall's 764 they take two sheets; at 758 they take one, with 1.6 to spare. Four would have missed it by 0.4 — the kerf is the whole margin here.

On the 4 × 8 it is the back wall that sets the count: it is 764 wide so it lies along the sheet, and nothing else that long will share the remaining 1212. The fore-aft rails and the rest come out of the 452 × 2435 beside it.

🔴 One rule covers all of it: every rail stays whole and whatever meets it gives way. The only cutting a rail ever takes is screw holes.

5 — Order

  1. Laminate all five legs, with a spacer in every slot. Cut each spacer from the stock that will fill that slot, wax or tape it so it does not glue in, and run it the full depth so the outer strips cannot bow inward under clamps.
  2. Trim the legs to 72 × 86 after the glue dries, and cut them to length — measuring from the mortise, not from an end, so the floor end takes the error.
  3. Cut the ledges and tenons on the three box legs.
  4. Build the two grids around the legs, then the two shear walls.
  5. Only then the box.

The mortise heights have to agree across the legs or the frame sits twisted — lay all five up on one flat surface with the spacers set off a single stop.
