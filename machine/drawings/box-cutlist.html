Sand ports

25.4 through both skins, 5 × 5 = 25 in each, one centred in every bay. Centres from the skin's edge, the same figures in X and Y — the box is square and the grid is the same both ways:

Centre129.5368.5605.5842.51081.5

The grid from above, with the bottom skin's ports

The steps are not equal — 239 at each end of the row and 237 through the middle, because the end bays are wider. Walking it as a constant pitch puts the far port in the wrong place.

The bottom port takes a rubber plug fitted from below with a pull tab underneath — nothing goes in from above and nothing is attached to it. Pull it to drain, push it back to seal. The sand bearing on one plug is only 76 grams, so friction is all it needs. Fill through the top ports once, at assembly, before the bed goes down: the bed cannot come out of the assembled machine.

The tee track bed covers all but the front row of five, which lands on the open apron. 6.35 Baltic birch over the apron on double-sided tape covers those — a cover, not structure, so it comes off the day a port is used.

Not on this list

The two blocks beside the back centre tenon. They are location pieces inside the box, carry no load, and their thickness is free — so they come out of the scrap bin rather than off a sheet. They must be 100 tall like a rib so both skins glue to them, and no thicker than 66.5, which is the clear space each side of the tenon.

Notches

Egg crate, continuous both ways, half-depth notches at every crossing. Every notch is 18 wide and 50 deep, and all the ribs of one direction are identical — so the saw is set once per direction and the whole set runs through it.

Notch depth exactly half — a set cut slightly deep sits low and loses contact with a skin along its whole length. The fit is a slip fit, not a clearance: too tight splits the rib, too loose leaves no glue line.

One direction is notched from the top edge and the other from the bottom, and whichever way round you choose, keep it the same for every rib in that direction. Positions below are from either end of the piece — the run is symmetric, so a rib fitted backwards still fits.
