WCS ORIGIN — THE SAME ON EVERY SHEET, unless that sheet says otherwise.
It is the FRONT-LEFT-TOP corner of the part as clamped. X along the longest dimension, Y across it, Z = 0 at the top face and negative into the material. All coordinates positive in X and Y, absolute, never chained.

Three sheets override this and say so in their own header: Sheets 1a and 1b stand the plate up, so X runs across the 200 and Y up the 301.625; Sheet 2d is clamped the short way on 2a’s origin. Standing rules for every plate in this pack
  • One WCS origin per plate, never chain dimensions. Each drawing names its origin corner; every hole is an absolute X/Y from it. On a manual mill with a DRO the operator types absolute coordinates, and chained dimensions accumulate error.
  • Lay the extrusion bolt patterns out by scribing. Two lines, four stations ticked along each — that is the eight-hole pattern at a beam end.
  • 9 mm clearance on M8 wherever a plate meets extrusion. That is the repo's deliberate figure — the joint works by friction, not bearing, and the float is wanted.
  • The plate is the drill guide for its own plywood tongue. No tongue on the torsion box is ever drilled to a coordinate. Bolt the machine up, set it on the box, and spot every tongue hole through the plate that secures it — the rear plate drills the back tongue, each outboard plate drills its own side tongue. Nothing downstream has to agree with the coordinates on these sheets, because the mating hole does not exist until the steel is in place.7.8
  • One fastener through every plywood tongue, the same stack everywhere: M8 × 30 flange bolt, head on the plate — through the plate, through the 19 mm baltic birch tongue, through an M8 fender washer, into an M8 acorn nut. The flange is the bearing face on the steel, the fender washer spreads into the plywood, and the acorn nut caps the thread on the outboard face where hands and hoses go past.7.9

7.8 — The plywood tongues are never drilled to a coordinate, and the drill guide is always the plate being secured. Not a template, not a story stick, and not a set of numbers off these sheets: the plate itself. Assemble the machine, set it on the torsion box, and spot each tongue through the holes of the plate that bolts to it — this rear plate drills the back tongue (X1–X8), and each outboard plate drills its own side tongue (K1–K7 on Sheet 9). That holds for every tongue on the box without exception, which is why the rule also sits in the standing-rules box at the front of the pack rather than only here. So the eight X stations here only have to clear obstructions and spread the load — nothing downstream has to agree with them, because the mating holes do not exist until the steel is bolted up and sitting in place. That is why 150 is a decision rather than a measurement, and why a hole landing a millimetre off costs nothing. It is the same logic as R5–R8 on Sheet 5: a hole you transfer needs no coordinate and fits better than one you compute.

7.9 — The tongue fastener, and it is the same one at every tongue hole. From the plate side: an M8 × 30 flange bolt, then the plate, then the 19 mm baltic birch tongue, then an M8 fender washer, then an M8 acorn nut. The ø9 holes in plate and tongue both are clearance, so this is a clamped joint, not a bearing one, and the two washer faces are doing the work: the bolt's own flange bears on the steel and the fender washer spreads the clamp over enough plywood face that the nut cannot pull into it. The acorn nut goes on the outboard face, the side you walk and reach past, and it is the reason the bolt runs this way round rather than head-outboard — a capped nut leaves no exposed thread to catch a hose or a knuckle.

⚠ CHECK THE GRIP LENGTH AGAINST THE PLATE BEFORE BUYING ONE BOX OF BOLTS. A flange bolt's 30 is measured under the flange, and the two plates are not the same thickness. Through the outboard plate the stack is 3.429 + 19 = 22.43 plus the fender washer, which leaves the better part of 6 mm in the nut. Through this rear plate it is 6.35 + 19 = 25.35 plus the fender washer, and an M8 × 30 has only about 3 mm of thread left to enter the acorn nut — an acorn nut is closed, so there is no running it down further. Measure the fender washer you actually have and expect the rear plate to want a longer bolt than the side plates do.
