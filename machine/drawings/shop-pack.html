This pack is deliberately incomplete, and the gaps are the point. Rows shaded amber are dimensions that do not exist anywhere in the repo. They have not been invented here. Every amber row is a thing to measure or decide before the trip, and the plate it sits on cannot be drilled until it is filled in.
Standing rules for every plate in this pack
  • One datum per plate, never chain dimensions. Each drawing names a datum corner; every hole is an absolute X/Y from it. On a manual mill with a DRO the operator types absolute coordinates, and chained dimensions accumulate error.
  • Take the mating hardware, not just the plates — bearing blocks, ball nut housing, BK12, BF12, and an offcut of the 30-6060 with its corner bores. Every pattern gets checked against the real part while still standing next to the mill.
  • Extrusion corner-bore patterns are transfer-punched, not dimensioned. Those bores are extruded features whose position tolerance is looser than anything drilled in plate. A punch through the real profile beats a coordinate every time.
  • 9 mm clearance on M8 wherever a plate meets extrusion. That is the repo's deliberate figure — the joint works by friction, not bearing, and the float is wanted.
  • Close-fit holes for rail one, oversize for rail two by about a millimetre, on any plate carrying two parallel rails.

Sheet 5 — Y nut doubler block and sole bracket (quantity 2 of each)

Added 2026-10-02 · two new parts per gantry end, both flat plate · they carry the inverted Y ball nut into the bottom of the X end plate

Why these exist. The X end plate now runs down to −182.5, level with the downward-facing mounting face of the Y ball nut. The doubler bolts to the plate's outer face to give a 25.4 mm (1") wide seating at the bottom; the sole bracket bolts up into that seating and reaches outboard to the nut. The screw axis is 72.7 mm from the plate's inner face — 12.7 plate + 30 rail stack + 30 half beam — so the nut's flange never comes near the plate and this is a cantilever, not a strip.

Nothing mates to either, so round them to whatever is convenient. 46 rather than 40 on purpose: 40 would match the nut face exactly but leaves only 8 mm of edge beside an M5 at ±12, about 1.45 D. Pack list

  • All four plates, plus the 12" square uncut if the taper is still open
  • Mating hardware: HGH20 bearing blocks, ball nut housing, BK12, BF12
  • An offcut of the 30-6060 with its corner bores — this is the transfer-punch master for every 8-bolt and 4-bolt extrusion pattern in the pack
  • The two Z spacer blocks, to be faced 1 mm — BOTH IN ONE SETUP, so they finish identical. Take the ball nut housing with them: the 1 mm is measured against that part
  • Transfer punches, M8 and M5
  • The coupler (25 mm OD, checks every ø35 bore)
  • A rail offcut, for checking the rail-hole pitch against the real part
The marginal cost of one more plate on the visit is an hour; a second trip is a weekend across the state. That is the whole reason this pack exists — so take anything plausible, drilled or not.
