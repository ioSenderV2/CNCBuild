How to read the sheets

  • WCS origin is the FRONT-LEFT-TOP corner of the part as clamped, X along the longest dimension, Y across it, Z 0 at the top face. Each sheet states what X 0 and Y 0 mean on that part, and the three sheets that override this say so in their own heading.
  • Coordinates are absolute, never chained. A row with several figures in X and several in Y is that grid crossed — the Qty column is the count.
  • The ø column is the drill. The Op column carries depth, counterbore and anything about how the hole is set out.
  • Refs tie the table to the drawing — every ref in a schedule is tagged on that sheet's figure.
  •     An amber row is a dimension that does not exist yet. Do not drill it — the checklist at the back says what it waits on.
  • Edge-bored rows are not on the drawing. They run into an end face; their X cell is a depth.

Pack list

  • All four plates, plus both fin pieces uncut if the taper is still open — they are 11 7/8" and each fin is its own piece now, not two halves of one square
  • The two Z spacer blocks, to be faced 1 mm — BOTH IN ONE SETUP, so they finish identical. Take the ball nut housing with them: the 1 mm is measured against that part
  • The coupler (25 mm OD, checks every ø35 bore)
  • A rail offcut, for checking the rail-hole pitch against the real part
  • A scriber and a centre punch, for the beam-end patterns
The marginal cost of one more plate on the visit is an hour; a second trip is a weekend across the state. That is the whole reason this pack exists — so take anything plausible, drilled or not.
