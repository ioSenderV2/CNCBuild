This pack is deliberately incomplete, and the gaps are the point. Rows shaded amber are dimensions that do not exist anywhere in the repo. They have not been invented here. Every amber row is a thing to measure or decide before the trip, and the plate it sits on cannot be drilled until it is filled in.
Standing rules for every plate in this pack
  • One datum per plate, never chain dimensions. Each drawing names a datum corner; every hole is an absolute X/Y from it. On a manual mill with a DRO the operator types absolute coordinates, and chained dimensions accumulate error.
  • Take the mating hardware, not just the plates — bearing blocks, ball nut housing, BK12, BF12, and an offcut of the 30-6060 with its corner bores. Every pattern gets checked against the real part while still standing next to the mill.
  • Extrusion corner-bore patterns are transfer-punched, not dimensioned. Those bores are extruded features whose position tolerance is looser than anything drilled in plate. A punch through the real profile beats a coordinate every time.
  • 9 mm clearance on M8 wherever a plate meets extrusion. That is the repo's deliberate figure — the joint works by friction, not bearing, and the float is wanted.
  • Close-fit holes for rail one, oversize for rail two by about a millimetre, on any plate carrying two parallel rails.

🔴 Why 116 mm centres and not 144. The M8 column at 50 mm is only 8.5 mm from the M5 column at 41.5 mm. An M5 counterbore is ~Ø10 and an M8 thread is Ø8, so at the same height those two features physically overlap — there is no web at all. The clearance has to be bought vertically. At 144 mm centres the inner M8 rows would land at ±59.5 against M5 rows at −59.2 and +56.8: a direct hit at both ends, and it would have been found at the mill. Holding 12 mm centre-to-centre in 2D confines the clamp mid-height to 55.2–60.8 mm; ±58 is the middle of that window and gives a worst case of 14.1 mm centre-to-centre, about 5 mm of web.
What 116 mm buys. It costs ~35% of the angular stiffness against the unobtainable 144, but it is still 37% better than clamping within the 154 mm block run. And at ±70.5 all four M8 rows sit inside the ±76.2 mm of the existing 6" spacers — every bolt over supported metal, with no block spreading, no travel spent and no spacer re-cut.

Do not lengthen the spacers. Raised repeatedly and rejected: an unbolted spacer end supports in compression only, and with zero overhang left there is nothing for it to support. The collision is between two features in the plate, which spacer length cannot affect.
Check the row measurements against the real spacer before drilling. The 17 and 133 are not quite symmetric about the spacer midpoint (59.2 versus 56.8), which is either measurement slop or a spacer that is not centred on its block pair. The 12 mm rule holds either way, but re-measure at the mill rather than trusting this sheet.

🔴 The M8 tapped holes and the M5 counterbores share the plate's front face. Both are entered from the clamp side, so this is a layout constraint, not a depth one. An M5 socket head counterbore is about Ø10 and the M8 thread major is 8, so bare non-overlap is 9 mm centre to centre — use 12 mm as the working minimum so the web survives the tap. Depth needs no separate check: the M5 counterbore leaves ~7.7 mm under the head while the M8 taps 10 mm into 12.7, so if they clear on the face they never meet.
