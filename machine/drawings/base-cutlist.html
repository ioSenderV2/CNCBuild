Base — sheet layout

58 pieces off 3 sheets of 18 mm Baltic birch. Millimetres throughout. Which sheet each piece comes off, and nothing else — the parts list is chapter 1 of base-construction.html.

Leg strips are ripped at 90 and trimmed to 86 once the lamination is dry, so every piece cut from strip stock — fillers and foot spacers included — is listed at 90 wide.

The glue-up spacers are not in this list. One set holds a leg's slot open while it is clamped and comes straight back out to be used on the next leg, so it is one set cut from rail offcut, waxed or taped, not a part of the base.

Kerf 1.6, edge trim 1.6 — one pass off a factory edge and nothing more, so a usable 4 × 8 is 1216 × 2435.2.

The parts list is not here — it is chapter 1 of base-construction.html, grouped by assembly, because that is where it gets read. This document is the sheets: which one each piece comes off.

How it divides across the sheets

Packed for the fewest sheets, not for a tidy offcut. Loading each sheet from one end keeps the remainder in a single piece, which is worth having — but on this mix it cost a whole extra sheet, and the sheet is the expensive thing. So the placements below are the packer's, the offcut comes back as several pieces rather than one, and a nest you place by hand can beat both.

🔴 The rear shear wall takes a band of its own with nothing beside it. Its 1216 length IS the sheet's usable width, so there is no room for a neighbour and its two ends are the trim cuts.
