How to read the drawings

  • Units are millimetres and the origin is the lower-left corner of every drawing, marked with a red dot. Inch equivalents are printed alongside for reference only — work to the millimetre figures.
  • Hole offsets from that origin are printed on each page — the X values across the top, the Y values down the left side, each on a leader from its own tick. They are there to read the drawing by; the DXF is the authority and the offsets are taken from it.
  • R12 DXF, lines, circles and arcs only. No polylines, no splines, no blocks. Outlines are closed.
  • Layers: OUTLINE is the cut profile, HOLES are the holes, NOTES and PARTS are text and are not to be cut.
  • Where a drawing says STOCK, the material is supplied and the outline drawn IS that piece — the job is the holes, and any excess falls outside them. Where it says MATERIAL, the shop supplies the plate and the outline is the part to cut.
  • Holes called out as edge-bored are not drawn. They run into a part's end face rather than through it, and are drilled here.
  • Where two parts share one piece, both are drawn in position on it with the kerf allowed between them.

What would help on the quote

  • Price per drawing, and separately the material where you are supplying it.
  • Say if any hole is below your minimum for the thickness — a pilot that gets opened out here is fine, and so is a scribed mark, so tell us which you would rather cut.
  • Note any outline you would rather nest differently. Nesting is yours to choose; the drawings do not assume one.
