14 holes per part, 42 across the three. Both rows share the same seven X stations — first at 50, then 150 pitch.
The two rows are 30 mm apart, and that separation is the dimension that matters. They are set out 15 either side of the strip's centreline, because they land in two T-slots that sit 15 either side of a beam seam. Hold the 30 between rows and keep each row straight along the length; the distance to a long edge is a consequence, not a requirement.
Hole position tolerance is loose, and row consistency is what is wanted. Every hole is a ø9 clearance for an M8 into a T-slot, and the nut slides along the slot to meet the bolt, so an individual hole being a few tenths out does not matter. What is wanted is that the seven stations agree with each other — and across all three parts, since they bolt to the same kind of beam. A wandering row still assembles and merely fights for an afternoon.

Notes for the quote

Material supplyThe shop supplies the 6061. Please say what thickness tolerance the stock holds — the part is bolted through, so thickness is not critical, but the customer needs the figure
NestingThree strips 1000 × 45 from one sheet. If a different strip width nests better and saves money, ask before changing it — the width is constrained by a bearing block clearance and is not free
FlatnessThese bolt down to an extruded aluminium beam along their whole length, so mild bow is pulled out by the fasteners. No flatness callout
MarkingNone needed. The three parts are identical and interchangeable
One thing is genuinely fixed: the 45 mm width. It is set by the gap between two bearing blocks the strip has to sit in. Wider will not fit the assembly at this thickness, and narrower loses bolt-head bearing. If the stock cannot be cut to 45, the customer needs to hear that rather than receive a substitute.
