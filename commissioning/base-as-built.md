# The base, as built

**Measured off the parts, not taken off the drawing.** Every figure here is his, with a tape or a
rule on the thing itself. Where it disagrees with the registry, this file is what the remaining
parts get cut to — the registry's figures are what the base was drawn at.

## What was measured

| | As built | Drawn |
|---|---|---|
| Base outer width, at the back | **1257** | {{BASE_W}} |
| Back shear wall | **1220**, and **tight against both side legs** — no air at either end | {{BACK_WALL_L}} |
| Rear tenon tabs, outside to outside | **1148** | 1151 |
| Rear tenon tabs, inboard face to inboard face | **1112** — the 1148 less the two tabs' {{TENON_CORNER_X}} | 1115 |
| Front cross rail outer laminations, as cut | **1225** | {{RAIL_CROSS_OUTER_L}} |

**The three readings agree to about a millimetre:** the outer width is 2 under, the wall fitting
tight says the span between the rear legs' outermost strips is 1220 against a drawn 1221 to 1223, and
the tenon span is 3 under. Nothing is wrong with the base — it is 2 to 3 narrow, consistently.

## The dry fit settles it, and it outranks the arithmetic

🔴 **The bottom skin was dry-fitted on its own onto all three tenons and dropped in**, the front pair
needing a little wiggling. **So no cutout gets relieved.**

That matters because the arithmetic said otherwise and was wrong. The two rear corner cutouts leave
**1113** of skin standing between their inboard edges, at 49 and 1162, and the measured tab span is
**1112** — one millimetre too much skin, which would have called for half of it off each corner. A
millimetre across a tenon pair is inside the error of the tape that measured it, and the skin on the
tenons is the instrument that answers the question. **The front pair wiggling is what
{{TENON_HOLE_CLEAR}} a side feels like**, not a sign of anything tight: that notch is
{{POST_W_X}} + 2 wide for a {{POST_W_X}} pair by design.

## What the remaining parts get cut to

| | |
|---|---|
| **Front cross rail outer laminations** | 🔴 **Cut to the measured span, NOT to {{BASE_W}} less two leg strips.** Go straight at the distance between the two FRONT corner legs' outermost strips' inner faces and cut to that. If the front matches the back it is about 1220, which makes the 1225 as cut four or five long rather than two. **This is the mistake the back wall caught** — the derived figure and the part disagreed by 3 |
| **The two rear corner tenon spacers** | **Fit each to its own gap**, which is about 13.5 rather than the drawn {{T_TENON_SPACER}}. Each rear tab has moved about 1.5 inboard, so a 12 spacer leaves 1.5 of air to the side web's inner face — and killing that slop is the entire job of the part. The 12.003 came from a stock 12 sheet miking at it, and that reason is gone once the gap is not 12 |
| **The back shear wall** | **Nothing. Tight is fine.** Its ends carry no load — it sits in a full-height rebate in all three rear legs and is screwed through its face — so the air it was drawn with was only ever a shadow line at the two rear corners |

## Two consequences that cost nothing

- **The box's tongues will stand about 1 proud of each leg's outer face** instead of flush. Cosmetic:
  the {{T_BOX_LEDGE}} shoulder bearing is untouched and the wall, skin, shoulder, post load line still
  lands square on it.
- **The rear tenon centres are about 1130 apart** rather than the drawn {{POST_SPAN_CORNERS}} less its
  two offsets. It matters nowhere — the box's position on the posts is not a machine reference, since
  the machine is referenced to the top skin.
