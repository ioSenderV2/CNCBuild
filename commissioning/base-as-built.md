# The base, as built

**Measured off the parts, not taken off the drawing.** Every figure here is his, with a tape or a
rule on the thing itself. Where it disagrees with the registry, this file is what the remaining
parts get cut to — the registry's figures are what the base was drawn at.

## What was measured

| | As built | Drawn |
|---|---|---|
| Base outer width, at the back | **1257** | {{BASE_W}} |
| Back shear wall | **1220**, and **tight against both side legs** — no air at either end | {{BACK_WALL_L}} |
| Rear tenon tabs, outside to outside | **1148** | 1151 |
| Rear tenon tabs, inboard face to inboard face | **1112** — the 1148 less the two tabs' {{TENON_CORNER_X}} | 1115 |
| Front cross rail outer laminations, as cut | **1225** | {{RAIL_CROSS_OUTER_L}} |
| Fore-aft rails, as cut | **1225**, trimmed to **1223** | {{RAIL_FORE_AFT_L}} |
| Central wall's reach forward of the back wall's INNER face, at its TOP edge, bolted to the rear middle leg with its back edge tight | **1217**, three times | — |

**The central wall's 1217 is the part, not a fit problem.** {{CENTRE_WALL_L}} is the panel measured
at that same top edge, and the binding edge **leans** — about 1223 at the bottom falling to 1218 at
the top — so the bottom reads longer than the top by design and the front end is not square. It costs
nothing: that end only has to stay well inside the front centre leg's {{POST_W_Y}} slot, and at 1217
it is about 62 deep there with nothing bearing on it. 🔴 **The edge that is NOT free is the bottom
one** — the fifth caster bears on it through {{CENTRE_WALL_PAD_L}} — and that is not the edge this
figure came off.

**The three readings agree to about a millimetre:** the outer width is 2 under, the wall fitting
tight says the span between the rear legs' outermost strips is 1220 against a drawn 1221 to 1223, and
the tenon span is 3 under. Nothing is wrong with the base — it is 2 to 3 narrow, consistently.

## The dry fit settles it, and it outranks the arithmetic

🔴 **The bottom skin was dry-fitted on its own onto all three tenons and dropped in**, the front pair
needing a little wiggling. **So no cutout gets relieved.**

That matters because the arithmetic said otherwise and was wrong. The two rear corner cutouts leave
**1113** of skin standing between their inboard edges, at 49 and 1162, and the measured tab span is
**1112** — one millimetre too much skin, which would have called for half of it off each corner. A
millimetre across a tenon pair is inside the error of the tape that measured it, and the skin on the
tenons is the instrument that answers the question. **The front pair wiggling is what
{{TENON_HOLE_CLEAR}} a side feels like**, not a sign of anything tight: that notch is
{{POST_W_X}} + 2 wide for a {{POST_W_X}} pair by design.

## What the remaining parts get cut to

| | |
|---|---|

Cut it to the same span and the front and back legs' outer faces end up the same distance apart, so the base is a rectangle by construction. One number for all four settles it in a way four individual measurements could not. **The central wall is NOT a fifth member of this group:** it runs to the front centre leg's slot, not to the cross rail, and matching the rails to it is a coincidence of two numbers rather than a shared rule |
| **The central wall** | **Nothing.** Tight at the back is what is wanted; the front end disappearing into the leg slot is the design |

Each rear tab has moved about 1.5 inboard, so a 12 spacer leaves 1.5 of air to the side web's inner face — and killing that slop is the entire job of the part. ## The tongue bolt holes

**The drawn pair** is {{TONGUE_BOLT_A}} and {{TONGUE_BOLT_B}} off the leg's back face, which is 17 and
51 into the tongue's {{TONGUE_IN_LEG}} — 17 of edge at each end, {{TONGUE_BOLT_Z}} up the rail.

**Two limits bound any pair, and they pull opposite ways:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Edge** at each end of the tongue's {{TONGUE_IN_LEG}} | as much as can be had — the drawn pair keeps 17 |
| **Separation**, centre to centre | at least **{{TONGUE_BOLT_SEP_MIN}}**, his — the {{FENDER_WASHER_D}} washers touch below that, and two washers riding on each other neither bear flat nor spread the clamp, which is the whole reason for a fender |

Widening the edge brings the pair together and narrowing it pushes them apart, so a pair has to clear
both at once. The drawn 34 apart clears the separation limit by 9.

## The corner legs' slots are filled, leaving a mortise at each grid

**Both corner legs have the slot filled with glued-in filler, with only a
{{GRID_MEMBER_H}} × {{GRID_MEMBER_T}} mortise left for the top and bottom rails** — his, as built,
and it is what the design asks for: the slot is CUT full height so no thin bridge of plywood is left
pretending to be the surface the box bears on, then closed by the foot spacer below the grids and the
filler block between them.

**So each rail sits in a true mortise with shoulders above and below it**, and those shoulders — the
blocks' end faces — are what stop the rail rotating in the vertical plane, by bearing rather than
through any bolt. That is why the bolt pair is spread **along** the tongue and not stacked up it, and
why a vertical or triangular pattern buys little here however good its arithmetic looks.

**The central wall is already bolted** in both centre legs, which is the same job done by a part that
was going in anyway — neither centre leg gets a filler.

## The pair as drilled, off the leg's FRONT face

**His, and it is the pattern the base is built to.** Both holes are set out from the leg's **front**
face — the face uppermost while the leg lies on its back on the wall, and the one the fore-aft rail
drops past into the slot.

The tongue runs **from** that face: its front end is the leg's front face and its back end is
{{TONGUE_IN_LEG}} in, against the wall.

| | Along the tongue, from the front face | To the tongue's back end |
|---|---|---|
| First, drilled | **21.5** | 46.5 |
| Second | **50** | **18** |
| Apart | **28.5** — clears {{TONGUE_BOLT_SEP_MIN}} by 3.5 | |

**So the nearer edge is 21.5 for the first hole and 18 for the second**, at opposite ends of the
tongue.

Both at {{TONGUE_BOLT_Z}} up the rail's {{GRID_MEMBER_H}}, which is the other axis and does not move.

**It clears both limits at once**, which is the thing worth checking and the reason to write it down:
21.5 and 18 of edge against the drawn pair's 17 and 17, for 28.5 of separation against 34. More edge,
less leverage, and both comfortable for a bolt doing location duty.

🔴 **The reference is the FRONT face, and getting that wrong is an 18 error in the dangerous
direction.** Set out 21.5 from the leg's BACK face instead and an 8 hole spans 17.5 to 25.5 while the
tongue's end face sits at {{T_BACK_WALL}} — it breaks out of the tongue's end and the bolt lands in a
scallop. The front face is also the face that is uppermost and reachable with a rule while the leg
lies on the wall; the back face is buried under it.

## One pattern at all eight tongues, and nothing else to do

**The same {{TONGUE_BOLT_A}} and {{TONGUE_BOLT_B}} at a front leg as at a rear one**, off the back
face there, which is that tongue's free end. A front leg has the same {{TONGUE_IN_LEG}} to work in —
the front cross rail's housed lamination takes the other end where the back wall does at the rear —
so the wood is the same joint twice and one pattern marks out both. **No leg needs anything relieved
and no rail needs shortening.**

**The ONLY laminations on this base are the caster pads, his.** Four
{{T_BACK_WALL}} × {{GRID_MEMBER_H}} outboard pads on the two LOWER fore-aft rails,
{{RAIL_LOWER_PAD_L}} long, and two on the central wall at {{CENTRE_WALL_PAD_L}}. Their job is the
caster plate: they bring each member out to {{POST_W_X}} so a plate lands on a full face and its
screws go into something solid. They butt a leg's face at each end and are **nowhere near a tongue
bolt**, which sits at the rail's mid-height inside the leg. **The upper rails get none** — there are
no casters up there.

**The front cross rail's INNER lamination is kept and cut short, his** — it stops
**{{RAIL_CROSS_INNER_END_CLEAR}}** shy of every leg face it meets, so each of the four pieces is
**{{RAIL_CROSS_INNER_SPAN}}** rather than butting. 🔴 **The NUT side is what sets that, not the
head.** A low-profile socket head stands only about 4.5 proud, but the far end of the same bolt
carries a washer, the {{BASE_BOLT_NUT_H}} flange lock nut and {{BASE_BOLT_PROUD}} of thread past it —
**{{RAIL_CROSS_INNER_END_MIN}}** in total — and which end a lamination meets depends on which way the
bolt went in, so the clearance covers the worse one. A clearance of 12 was tried and the model's
clash check refused it: the nut by 0.1 and the thread by 4.8, at all four pieces.

**The clearance is DRAWN, not declared**, so every rebuild of the model proves it instead of
tolerating it. If those pieces ever show up in the clash list again, the clearance has gone.

NOTE: **A "lamination" means two different parts in these files and that is what crossed the wires** —
the caster pads above, and the cross rail's inner half in the model. Say which when the word is used
on its own.

## The front centre leg bears through a 6061 bar

🔴 **There was no skin over that leg's shoulder, so the computed bearing area was bearing on
nothing.** {{SHOULDER_MID}} counts two strips of {{LEG_STRIP}} × 24 behind the tenon — but the bottom
skin's front notch **runs out to the skin's front edge**, which is what made it one opening instead of
two holes, and that leaves no skin standing over those strips. The two rear corners were never in
question: they sit inside closed cutouts with skin all round.

**His fix, in the part: a 110 × 20 × 10.2 bar of 6061, laid across the leg to bridge its two tenon
tabs.** The skin's front band lands on the bar instead of on air, and at 110 it reaches past the
leg's {{POST_W_X}} at both ends so the bearing is outside the slot rather than over it.

**It stands 0.7 proud of the two rear shoulders**, which measure about 9.5 against the bar's 10.2.
Nothing is done about it: three points cannot rock, so the box lands on all three and sits that much
nose-up over the fore-aft span, and the machine is referenced to the TOP skin rather than to how the
box sits on the legs.

**Aluminium is the right material here and not an indulgence** — the band it replaces is plywood
edge-on at about 1 MPa, and a 10 bar in compression over that span is nowhere near anything.

## The box closes the gap by hand, and the top skin is why

**Measured, his: with the box on its three points and NO TOP SKIN, moderate hand pressure on the
front left corner takes it down onto the top of the front left leg** — so it crosses the whole
{{BASE_BOX_GAP}}. The feet were all on the floor, so nothing was rocking; it is deflection in the box.

**That is the missing skin and not the box.** A torsion box works as two skins in tension and
compression with the ribs carrying shear between them; with one skin off it is an open tray, and
almost none of the designed stiffness is there yet. 🔴 **FOUR TEMPORARY SCREWS DO NOT MAKE A SKIN
EITHER** — the top works by being glued across the whole rib grid, so the box stays this flexible
until that glue-up, however firmly the sheet is held down at its corners.

🔴 **So this reading does NOT say anything about the finished mount, and must not be quoted as if it
did.** What it does say is that the check is worth repeating: **once the top skin is GLUED, lean on
each front corner again and watch the gap to the front corner leg.** If a corner can still reach a
leg top, the mount has a fourth and fifth point under load and assembly.md's silent failure is live —
the Y beams drift out of coplanar and nothing else shows it. If it cannot, the gap is doing its job.

## Two consequences that cost nothing

- **The box's tongues will stand about 1 proud of each leg's outer face** instead of flush. Cosmetic:
  the {{T_BOX_LEDGE}} shoulder bearing is untouched and the wall, skin, shoulder, post load line still
  lands square on it.
- **The rear tenon centres are about 1130 apart** rather than the drawn {{POST_SPAN_CORNERS}} less its
  two offsets. It matters nowhere — the box's position on the posts is not a machine reference, since
  the machine is referenced to the top skin.

## The order the front span gets set in

🔴 **There is no front span to measure until the fore-aft rails are in.** Each front leg is located
across the machine by ITS OWN fore-aft rail, because the rail fills the leg's {{GRID_MEMBER_T}} slot;
the rails are located at the back by the rear legs' slots, which the back assembly has already fixed.
So the rails carry the back's spacing forward and the front legs inherit it.

1. **Trim all four fore-aft rails to 1223.** One number for all four, which is what keeps the base
   from racking.
2. **Dry-assemble**: rails through the rear legs' slots, front legs onto the rails' front ends, bolts
   loose. 🔴 **BOTH grids' fore-aft rails go in, not just the upper.** A leg is held vertical by being
   captured at two heights in the same slot — square on the top rails alone and the bottom is still
   free to be out, and then the lower rail either will not go home or forces the leg when you make it.
   🔴 **Nothing glued at the front yet.** That reverses assembly.md's order, which has the three front
   legs pre-glued to the front cross rails — and it has to reverse, because the rail cannot be cut
   until the legs it will hold have told you the span.
3. **Square it AND plumb it — the diagonals cannot see vertical.** A leg can be plumb or leaning and
   the plan diagonals read the same. Set the two front legs so the front span matches the back, then
   take the **diagonals across the top of the upper grid**, and put a level on the leg faces. One
   rectangle plus plumb legs is the whole check.
   **Ignore the front centre leg here** — the rectangle is a four-corner job and that leg is located
   fore-aft by the central wall, not by the frame.

   🔴 **Lay the tape FLAT ON THE UPPER FORE-AFT RAILS and read corner to corner off the four corner
   legs' OUTER corners.** That plane is the only one that is clear, and it is clear by about 10 either
   way:

   | | Height off the floor |
   |---|---|
   | Central wall's top edge | 856 — the tape bridges over it |
   | **Upper fore-aft rails' top face — the tape lies here** | **864** |
   | The ledge, where every tenon starts | 874 — the tape passes under all three |

   At 864 each corner leg is still its full {{POST_W_X}} × {{POST_W_Y}} section with a square outer
   corner to hook on; the ledge and the tenon above it are cut {{BASE_BOX_GAP}} higher.

   🔴 **THE DIAGONAL AT THE LOWER GRID CANNOT BE TAKEN AND IS NOT WANTED.** The central wall runs
   from {{BASE_FLOOR_CLEAR}} up to its own top edge, so it crosses the lower grid plane and every plan
   diagonal in it. **What catches a leaning leg is the level on the leg faces**, not a second
   rectangle — which is why both halves of this step are listed, and the check is not weaker for
   having one diagonal pair instead of two.
4. **Measure the front span** between the two front legs' outermost strips' inner faces. It should
   read about **1220**, the same span the back wall occupies.
5. **Cut the cross rails' outer laminations to that** — **there are two, lower and upper, and they
   want the same span, so cut them together off one mark** — house them, and only then glue and screw
   the two front CORNER legs to them.
6. **Then bring the front centre leg up.** It slides fore-aft on the central wall, whose front end
   stops about 23 shy of the rail housing inside the leg's {{POST_W_Y}} slot — **that slack IS the
   adjustment**. Move the leg until its face meets the front cross rail, then fix it.
   🔴 **FORWARD IS FREE, BACKWARD IS NOT.** The bottom skin's front notch runs out to the skin's front
   edge, so the tenon moving forward has nothing to foul; moving it back has only about
   {{TENON_HOLE_CLEAR}} before the tab meets the notch's back edge. The dry fit dropped in at the
   leg's present position — mark where it sits and only ever take it forward from there.
7. **Cut the four inner lamination pieces last** — two per grid, one either side of the front centre
   leg, housed in nothing, so they follow everything else rather than setting anything.
   🔴 **THEY DO NOT BUTT THE LEG FACES.** Each end stops **{{RAIL_CROSS_INNER_END_CLEAR}}** shy of the
   face it meets, both ends, so **cut each piece to its own measured gap less 36** — the drawn figure
   is {{RAIL_CROSS_INNER_SPAN}} and the base is 2 to 3 narrow, so the gap is what to work off, not
   that number. Butted hard a piece lands on a bolt's washer and is held off the leg, which is the
   clearance's whole reason: see the {{RAIL_CROSS_INNER_END_MIN}} the nut side needs.

🔴 **If step 4 reads anything much off 1220, do not cut.** The diagonals are saying the base is not
square, and that is the whole reason for measuring at that point instead of trusting the figure.
